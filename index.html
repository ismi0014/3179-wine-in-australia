Where Australia's wine comes from

Where does it come from?

More than half of Australia's wine grapes are crushed in South Australia.

Which regions grow the most?

Three inland regions, the Riverland, Murray Darling, Swan Hill and the Riverina, crush more than two-thirds of Australia's wine grapes. Red circles mainly grow red grapes and gold circles mainly white.

Who grows what?

Each large block is a state, and each tile inside it is a grape. A bigger tile means more grapes crushed in 2023. South Australia dominates, led by Shiraz, while Western Australia grows more Sauvignon Blanc than any other grape. Queensland (under 0.05% of the harvest) is left out.

Which grapes are rising and falling?

Each square is one grape in one year. Darker means that grape made up more of Australia's harvest.

Where do red and white grapes grow?

Each dot is 1,000 tonnes of grapes. A dense belt of dots follows the Murray River, mostly gold white-grape dots, with red dots clustered around Adelaide and the Barossa. Smaller groups sit in the Yarra Valley, Tasmania and the south-west of Western Australia.

How do experts score our wines?

Each hump shows where a grape's scores cluster. A hump further right means higher scores, and a taller, narrower hump means reviewers scored it more consistently. Wine-red humps are red grapes and gold humps are white grapes.
